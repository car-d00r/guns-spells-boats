# Ship Basics
- Ships are based on monster stat blocks, they have CR.
- HP and damage are scaled down for speed by a factor of 1:5
- combat is on a hex grid, 1 hex = ft
- wind matters but is simple and optional
- ships may turn (optional), but they must move at least 1 square (or more depending on speed)
- initiative is rolled each round (maybe I do this maybe not) but only ships and solo creatures roll, passengers act on their ships initiative
- Action points are allocated at the top of each round by the captain
- Most actions require a ship to make an ability or skill check, characters can choose Action Stations on their turn to help with these checks.
- Customizing ships is easy and fun
## Ships Stats
just like monsters but slightly updated to be more applicable for an object.
- Strength: potency of attacks
- Dex: maneuverability
- Constitution: Fortitude and hp
- Charisma: Quality of the crew and their leadership (skills like to hit and full sail)
- AC: represents the resilience of the hull or agility and ability to dodge, how hard it is to hit and deal damage
- Speed: the number of 50ft squares they are able to turn in favorable winds. Max number of 50 mile spaces a ship can travel in a day (see wind and movement)
- HP Formula: number of damage before ship is derelict! Number is scaled down by a factor of 1:5 when compared to creature-scale encounters, see dice and scaling
- crew: min number of crew to operate the ship without being stressed. not tracked for ship combat but can be used for boarding, mob combat, and roleplaying
- max crew: max number of crew and passengers the ship can carry comfortably. number doubles for short voyages
- cargo: the number of cargo slots the hip has from 0-6, cargo might have different value in different ports
## Scaling
- Scale all dice rolls for the boat by 5 to put it in terms of creatures.
## Skill and Ability Test Difficulty
- default DC is 12, rolled by the acting captain unless another character is at a corresponding action station
- DM can modify the DC based on conditions like rough seas, weather, surprise, terrain, or a crew that is inexperienced, wounded, or frightened.
## Movement and position
- must move one space at least
- may rotate twice but only once per square
- ships in irons cant move but may rotate
- anchored ships cannot move
## Wind - probably skip this initially
- Wind may come from the north, south, east, or west of course, roll a d6, think of the square as a compass.
- The way your speed is affected depends on the point of the sail which is determined at the top of the turn, cant be changed during its turn (though the sail can be moved)
- with wind: full speed, close to the wind: half speed, in irons: speed zero, can turn
## Ramming
- Captain may spend all of its action points to declare a ram, at the end of the turn if the movement ends with any of the forward three sides touching another ship it may ram
- ships roll a die equal to their base hit die, ramming ship adds a 1 to its for roll per space it traveled this turn.
- Winning ship deals damage to the losing ship as though it had deal a critical hit with its primary attack
	- A brigantine (7d10+14 hp) rams into a sloop (6d8+6 hp). The brigantine travels 3 spaces before making contact. It will roll 1d10+3 versus the sloops 1d8. If the brigantine wins it will deal critical hit damage (2d10+3 ) to the sloop.
- If the movement fails to hit another ship you just lose all the action points
## Anchoring the ship
- Anchored, no movement or turning
- Drop anchor: costs all action points
- weighing the anchor: cost all ships action points. raises anchor. Ships speed is deduced to half this turn
- Cut and run, the anchor is lost and the crew needs a new one or the ship will continue to drift away
## Order of combat
- turns and rounds
- initiative is rolled for each ship and creatures on that ship all act on their ship's turn
- independent sea creatures and those not on a ship roll their own initiative
- for the purpose of the action economy combat represents 6 seconds but in the real world its like 30 seconds to a min
## Combat Step by Step
- Captain phase: captains choose their ship's actions and may roll to push the crew
	- each ship has a set number of action points.
	- any actions not taken up by the crew are rolled by the captain
	- PUSH THE CREW: Successful intimidation or persuasion check gives one extra action point to use this turn but is lost next turn if not used
		- cant do so two rounds in a row
- initiative: declare lookouts, lookouts or captains roll init
	- roll is made by the captain unless the ship has a lookout (ship rolls a d20 plus the ships dex)
- take turns: each ship when its their turn takes actions. The players or crew of the ship may act in the order they see fit. 
	- Each may choose an action station based on the chosen actions
	- They may also choose a different action like a spell casting action
	- Ships and characters may also ready actions to any triggers before the ship's next turn
	- Repeat the steps
## A ship's turn
- move up to the distance of the ships speed 
- make two rotations
- take any actions the captain chooses
- after all characters have gone, the captain rolls for any action the characters didn't take (not in any temporal order)
- again characters may ready actions
- arc of fire: cannons range is a cone off the broadside of the ship
